# FDE Delivery Matrix: Financial Services Use Cases

This page compares the ten use cases for forward-deployed engineering (FDE) discovery, design, validation, and handover. The linked briefs define the intended scope. The matrices below are **proposed delivery guidance**, not evidence that a system, control, integration, or target already exists. Agree scope, ownership, jurisdiction, permitted data use, service levels, and acceptance thresholds with the client before implementation.

## 1. Input and output contract matrix

For each integration, agree the schema, source owner, data classification, freshness, delivery method, validation rules, retention, and authorized consumer. Record source references and versions wherever an output influences a decision.

| ID | Primary inputs and validation | Outputs and destination | Exceptions and human handoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Applications, policy records, underwriting guidelines, correspondence, and events; verify identity, required fields, product, and guideline version. | Source-linked application summary, missing fields, routed work item, and processing report to policy/underwriting workflow. | Conflicting documents, incomplete applications, and rule ambiguity go to underwriting staff; do not infer missing facts. |
| 02 | Approved scenarios, dated exposure snapshots, historical/forecast data, model parameters, and calculation rules; reconcile coverage, totals, timestamps, and scenario approval. | Scenario impacts, sensitivities, limit comparisons, exceptions, and reproducible run records to risk reporting after approval. | Missing exposures, reconciliation breaks, or unapproved assumptions block affected calculations or publication and go to risk owners. |
| 03 | Licensed prices, curves, instrument terms, ratings, research, positions, and strategy assumptions; validate identifiers, currency, conventions, quote age, and source rights. | Instrument comparisons, portfolio and scenario views, and source-linked research notes to analysts. | Missing, crossed, stale, or indicative quotes are labeled or excluded per desk policy; analyst resolves discrepancies. |
| 04 | Authorized public video URL/file, publication metadata, and rate-topic vocabulary; check source availability, permission, and version. | Timestamped transcript excerpts, citations, topic tags, summaries, and uncertainty flags to analyst review/search. | Low-confidence speech, inaccessible media, and ambiguous statements require review; distinguish quotation from interpretation. |
| 05 | Claim forms, coverage records, evidence, correspondence, and events; verify claim-policy linkage, required evidence, and jurisdiction. | Source-linked claim summary, missing-evidence flags, triage work items, status, and review record in claims workflow. | Conflicting evidence, exclusions, reopened claims, suspected fraud, and disputes route to an adjuster or specialist. |
| 06 | Licensed market data, instrument/issuer reference data, orders, mandates, limits, and venue information; validate account, instrument, order, market status, and pre-trade checks. | Validated/staged order details, control results, execution and allocation records, confirmation/settlement status, and reconciliation breaks to authorized trading and operations systems. | Limit breaches, stale prices, partial fills, venue failures, and settlement breaks route to the responsible desk or operations team; never bypass checks. |
| 07 | Permitted official publications, policies, entity/product inventories, control mappings, and obligation metadata; verify jurisdiction, source version, publication and effective dates. | Source-linked obligation candidates, changes, applicability questions, control mappings, and review tasks in the approved GRC workflow. | Conflicting, amended, withdrawn, or uncertain provisions go to legal/compliance; extraction is not an applicability decision. |
| 08 | Authorized transaction/account data, alert features, customer/device signals, rules, and approved case history; validate permissions, completeness, time ordering, and rule/model version. | Prioritized alerts, linked activity timelines, evidence summaries, investigator tasks, and case audit records in approved case systems. | Data gaps, ambiguous matches, high-risk alerts, and potential filings go to investigators; no automatic freeze or filing without authority. |
| 09 | Incident, policy, vehicle, location, provider availability, and service events; verify identity, coverage, location confidence, and immediate safety needs. | Eligibility/missing-data flags, authorized dispatch request, status notifications, service records, and claim work items to assistance/claims systems. | Unsafe situations, uncertain location/coverage, unavailable providers, or failed dispatch escalate to an operator; do not promise unconfirmed arrival or coverage. |
| 10 | Customer message, approved knowledge, authenticated session, and authorized account data; validate intent, session, permissions, source freshness, and required action details. | Grounded answer, governed service action result, secure human handoff, and audit record to the channel/contact center. | Unauthenticated, ambiguous, disputed, unsupported, or failed actions result in safe refusal or handoff; report success only after authoritative confirmation. |

## 2. Scalability and resilience matrix

Measure actual baseline and peak workload before sizing. The capacity tests below should cover normal and peak load, dependency failures, recovery, and cost. Set throughput, latency, queue age, availability, recovery time, and data-loss objectives with the service owner; none are implied by this page.

| ID | Workload and scaling dimension | Design and backpressure | Failure and recovery test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Burst applications, document pages, OCR/extraction, and reviewer queue by product/channel. | Queue and independently scale document processing; bound concurrency and prioritize by agreed service commitments. | Duplicate uploads, OCR or policy-system outage, and backlog replay: prevent duplicate work items, retain source references, and route unresolved cases. |
| 02 | Exposure count, scenarios, horizons, model runs, and simultaneous reporting deadlines. | Partition approved calculations by portfolio/scenario where valid; isolate compute runs and checkpoint versioned inputs and outputs. | Fail a partition or calculation engine mid-run; rerun deterministically from the same snapshot and block incomplete results from publication. |
| 03 | Instruments, quote updates, users, and analytics requests during market peaks. | Separate market-data ingestion from analysis; cache only within license/freshness limits and bound expensive scenario queries. | Provider delay or disconnect: surface data age, prevent stale data being mistaken for live quotes, and recover source synchronization. |
| 04 | Video duration, audio quality, publication bursts, transcription concurrency, and index size. | Separate retrieval, transcription, indexing, and review queues; limit concurrency and retain segment checkpoints. | Inaccessible or changed source, transcription failure, or indexing outage: retry within policy, flag gaps, and avoid publishing incomplete summaries as verified. |
| 05 | Claims/events and evidence volume, catastrophe surges, and adjuster backlog. | Scale intake and document workers separately; apply priority and capacity controls agreed with claims operations. | Claim-system outage, duplicate event, and surge backlog: preserve claim linkage and provide visible pending status without duplicate decisions or payments. |
| 06 | Order rate, market-data events, venue connections, allocations, and reconciliation volume at peak trading. | Isolate low-latency pre-trade/OMS paths from asynchronous post-trade reconciliation; enforce bounded queues and sequencing. | Venue disconnect, timeout, partial fill, and replay: reconcile authoritative order state before retrying; never create a duplicate order. |
| 07 | Sources, jurisdictions, document versions, change bursts, and review tasks. | Use incremental source/version ingestion and deduplicate change events; partition review by jurisdiction and owner. | Source unavailable, amendment retracted, or delayed update: show last successful check and version; queue review rather than silently marking coverage complete. |
| 08 | Transaction/event rate, alert spikes, feature joins, case volume, and investigator capacity. | Isolate monitoring ingestion from case enrichment; bound queues, prioritize per approved policy, and monitor lag and dropped events. | Late/out-of-order transactions, feature-store outage, or alert surge: reconcile ingestion gaps and preserve alert provenance; escalate backlog per policy. |
| 09 | Calls/incidents, concurrent geolocation and provider requests, regional weather events, and dispatch capacity. | Scale intake and provider lookup separately; prioritize safety, bound retries, and expose provider/queue availability. | Provider API timeout, unavailable fleet, or duplicate dispatch request: confirm authoritative dispatch state and escalate to an operator. |
| 10 | Concurrent sessions, message rate, retrieval/API latency, contact-center capacity, and token cost where applicable. | Scale conversation, retrieval, and channel adapters independently; limit sessions/requests and reserve capacity for handoff. | Identity, knowledge, core-banking, or contact-center outage: fail safely, show clear status, and transfer or offer an approved recovery path; never repeat an uncertain transaction blindly. |

## 3. Security, privacy, and control matrix

Apply client-approved identity, encryption in transit and at rest, secret management, network controls, least privilege, audit logging, retention, and incident response to every use case. Validate legal basis and data residency with the client; avoid sensitive data in logs and use approved synthetic or de-identified evaluation data where possible. The table highlights additional use-case-specific controls.

| ID | Sensitive assets and access | Additional controls and approval boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Identity, application, policy, and possibly sensitive underwriting data; restrict by role and case. | Minimize extracted fields; protect source documents and retention; log reviewer overrides; reserve underwriting and issuance decisions for authorized staff. |
| 02 | Portfolio exposures, financial forecasts, scenario assumptions, models, and results; restrict by entity and risk role. | Version and approve scenarios, data, models, and code; separate model development, validation, and reporting permissions; prevent unapproved publication. |
| 03 | Licensed market data, positions, strategy, and research; enforce provider and desk entitlements. | Preserve source/time lineage and license restrictions; separate research from order handling; do not expose strategy or imply executable prices. |
| 04 | Source media, derived transcripts, summaries, and access credentials for retrieval services. | Restrict retrieval to permitted sources; respect content terms and copyright, including transcript redistribution; preserve provenance and protect against untrusted source content. |
| 05 | Claimant identity, policy, evidence, and potentially health information; restrict case access and purpose. | Apply sensitive-data minimization and retention; audit evidence and decision changes; separate recommendation from claim denial, settlement, and payment authorization. |
| 06 | Orders, positions, mandates, limits, venue credentials, and execution records; enforce desk/account entitlements. | Segregate duties, enforce approved pre-trade limits and surveillance, protect order integrity, and require authorized execution; reconcile rather than blindly retry uncertain submissions. |
| 07 | Confidential applicability assessments, legal interpretations, entity inventories, and control gaps. | Restrict assessment access; preserve authoritative source versions and review rationale; require qualified interpretation before conclusions or remediation decisions. |
| 08 | Transactions, identity/device signals, investigation evidence, and sensitive monitoring logic. | Enforce purpose limitation and fine-grained case access; restrict disclosure of detection logic; audit investigator actions and require approval for freezes, disclosures, and filings. |
| 09 | Identity, vehicle, precise location, incident, and provider/service records. | Limit location sharing and retention to the assistance purpose; authenticate provider integrations; record dispatch authorization and escalation for safety-critical cases. |
| 10 | Session, identity, account details, conversations, and action tokens. | Authenticate and authorize each operation, use step-up where required, prevent cross-account access and credential leakage, treat retrieved/user content as untrusted, and confirm action status via the authoritative system. |

## 4. Fairness and inclusion matrix

Fairness is context-specific: identify potentially affected people, relevant outcomes, comparison groups, and the decision-maker before choosing metrics. Assess direct and proxy effects, accessibility, language, and operational service differences. Use sensitive attributes only where lawful, necessary, and approved; otherwise use approved alternatives or document the coverage limitation. Do not infer protected attributes without authorization.

| ID | Potential disparity or coverage concern | Proposed checks and response |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Extraction, missing-document flags, routing, or underwriting support may differ by language, disability accommodation, product, or applicant group. | Compare extraction errors, manual-review rates, processing time, and corrections across permitted groups; investigate document-quality and proxy effects; offer human review and remediation. |
| 02 | Portfolio coverage and scenario design may obscure exposure or impact differences across regions, customer segments, or products. | Check representation and data gaps by approved segment; compare scenario assumptions and impact distributions, and have risk owners document limitations; this is not an individual eligibility decision. |
| 03 | Instrument, issuer, region, and liquidity coverage may favor well-covered markets or omit relevant opportunities and risks. | Measure data completeness, quote freshness, and analytic error by permitted market/instrument segment; disclose coverage gaps and avoid treating missing data as a negative signal. |
| 04 | Speech recognition and topic extraction may perform unevenly across accents, speakers, audio conditions, or languages. | Compare transcription error, timestamp error, and missed rate statements across a human-reviewed sample; flag low confidence and route affected passages for review. |
| 05 | Claim triage, evidence requirements, cycle times, or support may vary for vulnerable claimants, languages, accessibility needs, or permitted demographic groups. | Compare routing errors, evidence requests, time to resolution, outcomes, and overrides where lawful; review comparable claim types and severity, investigate differences, and provide appeal/human review. |
| 06 | Instrument/issuer and venue coverage or exception handling may differ by market, liquidity, or client mandate. | Compare data quality, control exceptions, execution/reconciliation outcomes, and latency by approved segment; check against mandate and best-execution policy without relaxing controls. |
| 07 | Jurisdiction, language, or source coverage may leave some entities or obligations less visible. | Measure source and obligation coverage, extraction errors, and review delay by jurisdiction/language/entity; disclose gaps and prioritize expert review. |
| 08 | Alert thresholds, proxy features, and investigation capacity can create unequal false alerts, missed events, or customer impacts. | With approved data and governance, compare alert rates, false-positive/false-negative estimates, investigation times, and outcomes by relevant groups and channel; account for label bias and delayed outcomes, investigate and escalate disparities before threshold changes. |
| 09 | Provider availability, dispatch prioritization, location precision, language, accessibility, or disability needs may affect service. | Compare answer, dispatch, arrival, cancellation, and resolution outcomes by region and permitted customer/service groups; examine safety and availability context and provide accessible operator escalation. |
| 10 | Intent recognition, answer quality, authentication, and handoff may vary by language, dialect, disability, channel, or digital familiarity. | Compare task completion, error, refusal, authentication failure, and handoff success across approved cohorts and accessible channels; test assistive technology and provide a human alternative. |

For any group comparison, report sample sizes and uncertainty, use comparable populations and time windows, suppress small cells as required, and avoid treating correlation or a single aggregate parity metric as proof of fairness. Establish an owner, remediation path, and re-evaluation cadence; do not claim fairness where labels or lawful group data are insufficient.

## 5. Evaluation and acceptance matrix

Use an agreed baseline and held-out, representative test set. Separate development from evaluation data; use temporal separation where behavior or market conditions change. An authorized domain owner should review high-impact outputs and sign off on thresholds. Report numerator, denominator, sample size, segment, period, and uncertainty; measure quality alongside speed and cost.

| ID | Quality and outcome measures | Required evaluation cases and reference | Proposed acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Field-level precision/recall against reviewed applications; routing accuracy, correction rate, cycle time, and straight-through rate. | Incomplete or conflicting documents, policy-rule changes, and overrides; compare extraction to source and routing to underwriter decisions. | Agreed field/routing thresholds and no unauthorized decisions; recorded reviewer sign-off, exception handling, and audit trace. |
| 02 | Run completion, reconciliation breaks, calculation duration, benchmark variance, and review time. | Missing exposures, scenario revisions, boundary values, fixed-snapshot repeatability, and directional sensitivities; compare with approved independent calculations. | Reconciliations and benchmark tolerances approved by risk/model owners; reproducible run package and publication approval. |
| 03 | Identifier-match accuracy, quote freshness, calculation error, research correction rate, and analyst time. | Crossed/missing quotes, market closures, corporate actions, and day-count conventions; reconcile with approved analytics and source data. | Agreed match/freshness and calculation tolerances; source-linked analyst-reviewed results and explicit stale/indicative labels. |
| 04 | Word error rate, timestamp alignment error, citation coverage, rate-statement precision/recall, and reviewer corrections. | Accents, overlapping speech, technical terms, poor audio, updated/removed video, and ambiguous policy language; compare with reviewed video excerpts. | Agreed transcription/citation thresholds and verified material claims; uncertainty and source rights documented. |
| 05 | Extraction and routing accuracy, evidence completeness, claim cycle time, rework, and claimant contact resolution. | Conflicting evidence, exclusions, reopened cases, vulnerability, and fraud referrals; compare with adjuster-reviewed cases and source evidence. | Agreed segment-level thresholds, verified audit trail, and proof that decisions/payments require authorization. |
| 06 | Pre-trade exception rate, order-to-confirmation time, allocation breaks, settlement failures, and reconciliation accuracy. | Breached limits, stale prices, partial fills, cancellation, venue outage, and settlement exception; compare non-production outcomes with system-of-record results. | Control checks cannot be bypassed; agreed reconciliation and latency tolerances, tested recovery, and trading/operations approval. |
| 07 | Source coverage, change-detection precision/recall, mapping completeness, and expert review latency. | Amendments, withdrawn guidance, conflicting provisions, effective-date changes, and uncertain applicability; compare with official source history and expert-reviewed changes. | Agreed jurisdiction coverage and extraction thresholds; source version, qualified applicability decision, and unresolved gaps documented. |
| 08 | Alert precision, recall or missed-event estimates where labels permit, time to disposition, investigator workload, loss, and disparity indicators. | Temporal holdout with adjudicated outcomes; data gaps, benign lookalikes, channel shifts, evolving typologies, and threshold changes. | Risk/compliance-approved performance and fairness tolerances, documented label limitations, and verified human control over account actions and filings. |
| 09 | Time to answer/dispatch, arrival-time error, successful dispatch, first-time resolution, repeat contacts, and claim rework. | Unavailable provider, uncertain location, coverage exception, duplicate/failed dispatch, severe incident, and accessibility needs; compare with operations records. | Agreed service thresholds and safety escalation; dispatch state and notifications verified with operations and claims owners. |
| 10 | Task completion, grounded answer accuracy, authentication failure, successful handoff, complaints, and repeat contacts; report containment only alongside completion. | Ambiguous/adversarial prompts, outdated knowledge, account-specific requests, privacy leakage, accessibility, failed/uncertain transactions, and transfer failure. | Agreed intent-level thresholds, no unauthorized access or unconfirmed success in tested cases, and customer-service/security sign-off. |

Before release, run negative and failure-path tests in addition to aggregate metrics. Record defects, severity, owner, mitigation, residual risk, approval, and a rollback decision. A passing sample does not prove that untested failures cannot occur.

## 6. Architectural differences and integration boundaries

These are candidate patterns, not mandates for a particular cloud provider, model, or product. Implement only components needed by the approved workflow. The system of record remains authoritative; generated content and intermediate caches do not replace it.

| ID | Candidate data and control flow | Distinctive architecture and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Application/document intake -> classification and extraction -> policy-rule checks -> underwriting work queue -> policy system. | Document-centric asynchronous pipeline with field-level provenance and human approval; policy platform owns issuance. |
| 02 | Versioned exposure snapshot + approved scenario -> governed calculation engine -> reconciliation/driver analysis -> risk review -> reporting. | Batch/parallel numerical compute and immutable run lineage; generated explanations cannot replace the calculation or independent model validation. |
| 03 | Licensed market/reference feeds + positions -> normalization/freshness checks -> approved analytics -> analyst research view. | Read-oriented market-data and analytics architecture; no execution path, unlike 06. |
| 04 | Permitted public video -> audio/transcription segments -> timestamped search/extraction -> cited summary -> analyst review. | Media processing and citation alignment rather than transaction processing; untrusted source content remains separate from instructions. |
| 05 | Claim intake/evidence + policy lookup -> extraction/triage -> adjuster case workflow -> authorized decision/payment systems. | Evidence-centric case orchestration; policy coverage and payment systems remain authoritative, with adjuster control. |
| 06 | Order + market/mandate data -> pre-trade controls -> authorized OMS/venue -> fills/allocations -> confirmation and settlement reconciliation. | Stateful transaction workflow with strict entitlements, sequencing, and authoritative order-state reconciliation; unlike 03, interfaces can affect trades. |
| 07 | Official sources + versions -> change detection/extraction -> jurisdictional expert review -> GRC obligations and controls. | Versioned document/change graph and human interpretation; effective dates and withdrawn sources are first-class. |
| 08 | Authorized monitoring events -> approved rules/models -> alert grouping/evidence -> investigator case management -> approved escalation/reporting. | High-volume event processing plus restricted case workflow; model/rule versions and investigative access must be governed. |
| 09 | Incident + policy/location -> safety/coverage check -> provider availability -> authorized dispatch -> service events -> claim reconciliation. | Time-sensitive multi-party orchestration with location and provider dependencies; safety escalation takes priority over automation. |
| 10 | Channel/session -> identity and intent -> approved knowledge or authorized account APIs -> answer/action confirmation -> secure handoff. | Interactive, per-request authorization and session isolation; knowledge retrieval is separate from transaction execution and authoritative confirmation. |

## 7. FDE delivery and handover checklist

1. **Discover and bound:** identify client sponsor, domain owner, users, jurisdictions, current baseline, authoritative systems, in-scope actions, and decisions reserved to humans. Confirm whether each candidate integration and data source is available and permitted.
2. **Design the contract:** map data flows, classifications, source and destination owners, schemas, freshness, permissions, versioning, lineage, failure states, and human escalation. Record architecture decisions and dependencies.
3. **Set measurable acceptance:** agree workload profile and peak assumptions; numerical thresholds and denominators for quality, latency, capacity, resilience, fairness, and cost; representative datasets; and domain/security/risk sign-off owners.
4. **Build a bounded pilot:** use the approved environment and data, enforce decision and entitlement boundaries, instrument audit/operational telemetry, and provide visible exceptions, retries, and rollback. Validate side-effecting calls against authoritative state before any retry.
5. **Validate and review:** execute the use-case tests above, including security, accessibility, group-coverage limitations, load, dependency outages, reconciliation, and human handoff. Record evidence and resolve or explicitly accept residual risk before release.
6. **Operate and hand over:** provide a runbook with service ownership, alert thresholds, backlog and drift monitoring, incident contacts, recovery/rollback, access review, retention, change control, and periodic re-evaluation. Obtain business, operations, technology, security, and relevant risk/compliance approvals.

The matrices are discovery starting points, not legal, regulatory, investment, or model-validation advice. Do not place credentials, production customer data, or confidential client material in this repository.

### Suggested FDE stage gates for these capabilities

| Stage | Financial-client evidence to produce | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|
| Discover and select | Named business owner; bounded workflow and user; authoritative sources and systems; jurisdiction/data classification; baseline; decision and automation boundaries; candidate platform and deployment constraints. | Client owners approve the use case, measurable outcomes, data access, and human decision boundary. |
| Prove value | Time-bounded prototype or workflow slice; representative approved data; source-linked outputs; baseline-versus-pilot evaluation; failure, access, fairness, and cost observations; user feedback. | Agreed acceptance thresholds are met on the approved evaluation set, and unresolved risks have owners and dispositions. |
| Productionize and operate | Security and architecture approvals; production integration; monitoring and audit evidence; operational ownership, incident/rollback plan, change controls, and user training. | Required business, technology, operations, security, privacy, and risk/compliance approvals are recorded; service ownership and ongoing measures are in place. |

The stage gates above are proposed for this matrix; they are not represented as Deloitte’s published methodology or as a guarantee that a given platform satisfies client or regulatory requirements.
