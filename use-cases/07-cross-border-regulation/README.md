# Cross-Border Regulation

## Objective

Help teams identify, organize, and monitor regulatory obligations that may apply across jurisdictions, legal entities, products, and activities. Outputs support compliance professionals and are not legal advice or a substitute for authoritative interpretation.

## Discovery questions

- Which jurisdictions, regulators, entities, products, and obligations are in scope?
- Which official sources and internal policy owners are authoritative?
- How are effective dates, amendments, conflicts, and applicability decisions tracked?
- Which obligations map to controls, evidence, owners, and reporting deadlines?

## Inputs and outputs

- **Inputs:** Official regulatory publications, internal policies, entity and product inventories, control mappings, and obligation metadata.
- **Outputs:** Source-linked obligation records, change alerts, applicability questions, control mappings, and review tasks.
- **Integrations:** Regulatory content services, GRC platforms, policy repositories, and issue or workflow management systems.

## Candidate workflow

1. Ingest permitted regulatory sources with jurisdiction, publication, and effective-date metadata.
2. Detect changes and link extracted statements to exact source passages and versions.
3. Present potential impacts for review by jurisdictional compliance or legal owners.
4. Record applicability decisions, rationale, affected entities, controls, and accountable owners.
5. Track remediation and evidence through the client-approved GRC process.

## Measures and validation

- Track source coverage, change-detection precision, review latency, overdue assessments, and mapping completeness.
- Compare extracted changes with expert-reviewed samples and official source histories.
- Test amendments, withdrawn guidance, conflicting provisions, effective-date changes, and uncertain applicability.

## Controls and FDE deliverables

Use authoritative sources, preserve provenance and version history, restrict access to confidential assessments, and require qualified human interpretation. Clearly distinguish extracted text from applicability conclusions.

Document jurisdiction coverage, source inventory, ownership model, review and escalation process, validation results, and limitations.
